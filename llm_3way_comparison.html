Which LLM is "smartest"? An in-depth read

Sharpe alone is misleading on this dataset (metric outliers, multiple-comparison inflation). A more useful question is "which LLM did the best research," meaning: who explored more thoughtfully, caught their own mistakes, and produced a credible distribution of results rather than one lucky leaderboard hit. Below I rank the three on each axis using strictly the measurements above.

Axis 1 — Discovery rate (how fast did good results emerge?)

GPT delivered a Sharpe ≥ 3 result on its first analyzed experiment (market_conditioned_client_lgbm, S=3.75 at wall_h ≈ 0). Opus reached Sharpe ≥ 3 at 1.13 h. Grok reached Sharpe ≥ 3 at 14.09 h — much later, after most of its run. By the equalize point, gpt had 22 realistic Sharpe-≥3 results, opus had 17, grok had 3. Winner: GPT.

Axis 2 — Productivity (throughput in the same wall time)

Grok ran 295 experiments to completion in the same 16.61 h that opus did 156 and gpt did 83. Conversion rate (analyzed / proposed): opus 50%, gpt 23%, grok 85%. GPT proposed 4× more than it analyzed — most of its strategist output never made it through the pipeline in this window. Winner on pure throughput: Grok. Winner on conversion efficiency: Grok again, but it gets the volume by writing shorter proposals (512 description chars vs opus 3083 vs gpt 1778).

Axis 3 — Quality distribution (does the median experiment work?)

Looking at median realistic Sharpe — a much harder signal than peak — gpt sits at 2.14, grok at 1.76, and opus at 0.87. Negative-Sharpe rates: opus 31/150, grok 35/153, gpt 8/71. GPT's analyzed experiments are systematically better — its median is above 2, twice the others. Winner: GPT.

Axis 4 — Research breadth (how much was actually explored?)

Unique model families touched: opus 22, gpt 18, grok 15. Opus explored the widest, gpt second, grok narrowest. Grok concentrated heavily on LSTMs (169 mentions in experiment names — more than any other family across all 3 runs) while opus and gpt spread effort more evenly. Winner on breadth: Opus. (Caveat: deeper exploration without convergence can also mean unfocused work — see Axis 5.)

Axis 5 — Per-experiment depth (effort and reasoning behind each proposal)

Average description and hypothesis sizes are stark: opus writes 3083-char descriptions and 666-char hypotheses, gpt 1778/295, grok 512/536. Opus puts ~5× more reasoning into each proposal than grok. Debrief sizes follow the same ordering — opus debriefs avg 13,453 chars, gpt 11,682, grok 6,090. Winner: Opus.

Axis 6 — Self-correction (did debriefs catch their own bugs?)

Regex-flagged share of debriefs containing "implausible" / "metric bug" / "leakage" / "outlier" / "degener[ate]" / "sanity fail" language: gpt 92%, opus 81%, grok 79%. Caveat: this can over-count — "leakage" appears in many debriefs as a check that was performed, not a bug actually found. But the ordering is consistent: gpt's analyzer pipeline writes the most self-critical debriefs. Winner: GPT.

Axis 7 — Iterative refinement (does later work build on earlier work?)

This is where grok is the only data point. The parent_id column for propose_variant chains exists only in the grok-branch DB. Grok has 60 child rows with max chain depth 3 — concrete evidence of strategist iteration on prior results. Opus and gpt likely iterated too (you can see this in their experiment names suffixed with _v2, _rerun, _aligned), but it isn't structurally tracked. No fair comparison on this axis.

Axis 8 — Steering quality (how well did each Conductor intervene?)

Conductor decision volume diverges by an order of magnitude: gpt 1651, opus 953, grok 210. The directive churn ratio (retires / directives) is striking: opus 1.18 — over-retiring, meaning many directives were issued only to be retracted; gpt 0.97 — near 1:1 issued-and-retired; grok 0.50 — issues few, retires fewer. Reading this charitably: opus and gpt actively re-steer, sometimes correcting prior steering; grok's Conductor barely steers at all. Reading uncharitably: opus and gpt are confused about what they want, grok is just absent. Park-then-unpark reversals are 0 for all three, which means none of them reversed a hard parking decision — once committed to dropping a row, all three stayed committed.

Axis 9 — Cost-efficiency (tokens per analyzed experiment)

Tokens-per-analyzed: opus 9M, gpt 20M, grok 3M. Grok is ~3× cheaper per analyzed than opus, ~9× cheaper than gpt. Winner: Grok.

Synthesis

If "smartest" means best research outputs per unit time — credible Sharpe values, high median, fastest discovery — GPT-5.5 wins. It hit Sharpe ≥ 3 within minutes (on its very first analyzed experiment), kept its median realistic Sharpe at 2.14, and produced 22 realistic Sharpe ≥3 results vs 17 from opus and 3 from grok. Its analyzer pipeline writes the most self-critical debriefs. Tradeoff: only 83 experiments analyzed in 16.6 h — about a third of grok's count — and ~5× more tokens per analyzed than grok.

If "smartest" means deepest thinking per proposal — well-developed hypotheses, broad mechanism coverage, thoughtful debriefs — Opus-4.7 wins. It writes 6.0× longer proposals than grok (3083 vs 512 description chars) and explores the widest set of model families (22 vs grok 15, gpt 18). Quality is in between (median Sharpe 0.87, peak realistic 4.73); each unit of work shows more reasoning behind it.

If "smartest" means throughput and efficiency — most experiments per hour, most analyzed per token — Grok-4.3 wins. It analyzed 295 experiments to opus's 156 and gpt's 83 in the same wall-time, at much lower token cost. The cost is depth: 512-char descriptions, 6,090-char debriefs (~half opus's). Its peak realistic Sharpe (3.45) sits below the other two (4.73, 4.98). Its Conductor engages thinly: 210 logged decisions vs opus 953 and gpt 1651, and 6 debrief reads in the whole window vs gpt 247 — steering off synthesis files rather than experimental ground truth.

No single LLM wins on all axes. The three models have distinct research personalities and the choice of which is "best" depends on what you weigh — peak quality (gpt), depth (opus), or throughput + efficiency (grok).

What this analysis does NOT establish. (1) Sharpe values from the harness backtest are subject to multiple-comparison inflation and metric-implementation bugs (the ≥5 filter is a coarse cutoff, not a calibration). A holdout-set re-run of each LLM's top-N rows through a unified audited harness would be needed for a definitive quality comparison. (2) "Self-correction" via regex is approximate — a debrief saying "we checked for leakage and found none" counts as a flag even though no bug was caught. (3) The Conductor-decision count includes some categories (set_priority, annotate) that are not strictly steering — but their distribution still characterizes how engaged each Conductor was. (4) Token tallies include reasoning_tokens for grok but not for opus / gpt (Bedrock and the older OpenAI Responses API run did not expose them as a separate field). The input+output sums are consistent. (5) Wall-time alignment treats the start of Phase 3 as the natural zero, but Phase 0/1/2 productive work also varies per LLM — that's not captured here.
